Does feedback help students develop communication?

Feedback addresses communication development, not just correctness

Why this matters

University contains many hidden rules: what “critical analysis” means, what a rubric is really asking for, when it is acceptable to ask for help, or what counts as “professional” behaviour. If those rules remain unspoken, familiarity can be mistaken for capability.

What this looks like

Common signs

  • Important expectations may remain implicit or be expressed only through insider terminology.
  • Students may be expected to infer processes or disciplinary conventions through trial and error.
  • Assessment can feel like a test of hidden rules rather than a supported opportunity to learn.

Put this into practice

Start with the student experience

Before changing the design, look at what the issue feels like from the student side. Student voice can help distinguish a genuine learning requirement from friction created by the way the course is organised.

  • “Everyone else seems to know rules nobody has explained.”
  • “I understand the topic, but not what I am being asked to do.”
  • “I keep learning how university works only after I have already made the mistake.”

Use UQ Students as Partners to bring student voice into the review

A practical example

Connect it to existing UQ work

Use existing UQ guidance, services and initiatives as the starting point rather than building a parallel process.

  • Creating and using rubrics — Clarify criteria, standards and the qualities students are expected to demonstrate.
  • Feedback for learning — Design feedback that supports students to make a useful next move.
  • Assessment Security Guidelines — Use clear expectations, rubrics and annotated exemplars to reduce confusion.

Copy and adapt a starting point

Use these as editable starting points. Replace the bracketed text, adjust the language for your discipline and only keep wording that matches the actual learning design.

For this assessment, we aim to use feedback to help students develop disciplinary and academic communication, not only correct errors. The learning outcomes being assessed are [insert outcomes]. If the task, criteria or process is unclear or creates an avoidable barrier, contact [course contact/channel] before [date].

Review prompt: Does feedback help students develop communication? • Comment on how effectively meaning is being made, not only surface errors. • Prioritise the next most useful improvement rather than listing every issue. • Where possible, create a later opportunity to apply the feedback.

Course review note — Feedback for academic communication: We will use feedback to help students develop disciplinary and academic communication, not only correct errors. Owner: [name/role]. Change to make: [action]. Evidence we will check: [student feedback / participation / assessment pattern / accessibility check]. Review point: [date or teaching period].
