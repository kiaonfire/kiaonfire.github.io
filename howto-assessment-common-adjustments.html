Assessment · Quick how-to

Build common adjustments into assessment

Anticipate common disruptions and access needs so students do not have to repeatedly prove that their circumstances are legitimate.

Framework practices used here

This how-to pulls together several parts of the Inclusive Teaching Framework rather than treating the issue as belonging to one area.

EquityAccessibility

Quick steps

1
Identify the extensions, alternatives or access adjustments students request most often.
2
Check which can be built into the task without changing the learning outcome.
3
Publish flexibility and recovery options before students need them.
4
Keep individual adjustment pathways for needs that cannot be met universally.

Use these framework practices

Equity of opportunity

Build common adjustments into assessment

Design short extensions, alternative participation routes or recovery options as normal features where appropriate.

Accessibility

Offer meaningful choice in assessment

Offer choice where it supports engagement and does not compromise outcomes.

Accessibility

Offer accessible ways to receive feedback

Offer feedback in more than one mode where feasible, such as written comments plus an audio summary.

Equity of opportunity

Build in drafts and checkpoints

Add low-stakes checkpoints that make progress visible before the final due date.

Example in practice

A course combines a simple short-extension pathway, an early checkpoint and equivalent format options so common disruptions do not require extensive disclosure.
