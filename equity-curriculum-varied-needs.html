Do I design for common barriers?

Course design reflects anticipated cohort needs, drawing on past and current enrolment data

Why this matters

The same course requirement can land very differently depending on time, money, care, disability, work, housing or confidence. Equity asks whether students with different lives have a real opportunity to participate and succeed, rather than simply receiving the same rule.

What this looks like

Common signs

  • Requirements may assume spare time, predictable schedules, money, transport or uninterrupted study conditions.
  • Support or flexibility may exist only after a student discloses private circumstances.
  • One difficult week can become a major barrier when recovery is not built in.

Put this into practice

Start with the student experience

Before changing the design, look at what the issue feels like from the student side. Student voice can help distinguish a genuine learning requirement from friction created by the way the course is organised.

  • “This course assumes university is the only thing happening in my life.”
  • “I can technically enrol, but the way this is organised makes participation fragile.”
  • “Asking for flexibility feels like admitting that I am not the right kind of student.”

Use UQ Students as Partners to bring student voice into the review

A practical example

A course with a large working-student cohort releases its full semester map before week 1 and reduces unnecessary on-campus spread.

Connect it to existing UQ work

Use existing UQ guidance, services and initiatives as the starting point rather than building a parallel process.

  • Inclusive practice — Consider how different circumstances, backgrounds and resources shape participation.
  • Online teaching — Use online and asynchronous design to create flexibility in time, place and participation.
  • Open education — Reduce cost barriers by using and creating openly licensed teaching resources.

Copy and adapt a starting point

Use these as editable starting points. Replace the bracketed text, adjust the language for your discipline and only keep wording that matches the actual learning design.

This course has been designed to design around common barriers experienced by students with different lives, resources and responsibilities. If you notice an avoidable barrier or an important perspective that is missing, tell us through [contact/channel] so the teaching team can review it.

Review prompt: Do I design for common barriers? • Review common barriers from prior offerings and likely cohort patterns before the semester begins. • Use those patterns to decide where early clarity, flexibility or scaffolding is most needed. • Treat differences in cohort composition as design information, not anomalies.

Course review note — Designing for diverse student needs: We will design around common barriers experienced by students with different lives, resources and responsibilities. Owner: [name/role]. Change to make: [action]. Evidence we will check: [student feedback / participation / assessment pattern / accessibility check]. Review point: [date or teaching period].
