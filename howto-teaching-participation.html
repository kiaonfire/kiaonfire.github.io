Teaching · Quick how-to

Increase participation without putting students on the spot

Create lower-risk ways to contribute so participation does not depend on speed, confidence or comfort speaking publicly.

Framework practices used here

This how-to pulls together several parts of the Inclusive Teaching Framework rather than treating the issue as belonging to one area.

AccessibilityRespectLanguage

Quick steps

1
Explain how participation will work before asking for it.
2
Use think time before inviting responses.
3
Offer different contribution routes where the activity allows.
4
Avoid treating quietness or slower response as lack of understanding.

Use these framework practices

Accessibility

Offer different ways to participate

Offer more than one way to contribute, such as speaking, posting or sharing through smaller groups.

Accessibility

Build processing time into teaching

Pause after complex explanations and allow time to think.

Respect & representation

Set clear norms for respectful participation

Co-create or clearly state discussion norms early.

Language & literacies

Normalise unfamiliarity with academic language and norms

Invite students to ask about unfamiliar terms without embarrassment.

Example in practice

Students can respond through a shared document, small-group discussion or verbal contribution rather than one compulsory mode.
