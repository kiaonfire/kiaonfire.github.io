Can students practise before high-stakes assessment?

A practical Inclusive Teaching Framework action.

Framework practice

Language · Assessment

Low-stakes activities scaffold disciplinary communication

Back to Assessment

Why this matters

University contains many hidden rules: what “critical analysis” means, what a rubric is really asking for, when it is acceptable to ask for help, or what counts as “professional” behaviour. If those rules remain unspoken, familiarity can be mistaken for capability.

What this looks like

Common signs

  • Important expectations may remain implicit or be expressed only through insider terminology.
  • Students may be expected to infer processes or disciplinary conventions through trial and error.
  • Assessment can feel like a test of hidden rules rather than a supported opportunity to learn.

What students may experience

  • “Everyone else seems to know rules nobody has explained.”
  • “I understand the topic, but not what I am being asked to do.”
  • “I keep learning how university works only after I have already made the mistake.”

Steps to address it

1
Let students practise unfamiliar genres before they are graded heavily.
2
Use short, low-stakes opportunities to build vocabulary and confidence.
3
Treat these activities as part of the learning pathway, not add-ons.

Example in practice

Before a major oral presentation, students complete a brief low-stakes explanation activity and receive feedback on communicating to the intended audience.
