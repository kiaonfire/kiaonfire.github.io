Respect & representation · Assessment

Check cultural assumptions and hidden norms

Use this Inclusive Teaching Framework practice to identify a specific barrier, understand what it can feel like for students, and make a practical change.

Framework focus

Theme: Respect for, and representation of, cultures and identities

Area: Assessment

Back to Assessment

Inclusive Teaching Framework statement

Cultural assumptions and hidden norms about expression are identified and removed where irrelevant to outcomes

Why this matters

Teaching is never neutral. Choices about readings, cases, examples, guest speakers, classroom norms and professional pathways tell students whose lives and knowledge are considered normal, legitimate or peripheral. When students cannot recognise themselves or their communities in the discipline, participation can start to feel risky or pointless.

How the barrier can show up

  • The course may centre only a narrow set of identities, voices or professional pathways.
  • Students may encounter norms that quietly position some ways of being as more legitimate than others.
  • Participation can become more cautious when students expect to be stereotyped, ignored or made responsible for representing a whole group.

What it can feel like for students

  • “People like me do not seem to exist in this field.”
  • “We only appear when the course is discussing disadvantage or a problem.”
  • “To contribute here, I may need to ignore part of myself or explain my community to others.”

How to address it in practice

1
Check whether the task rewards one style of expression when that style is not the learning outcome.
2
Clarify which conventions are essential and which are inherited habits.
3
Rewrite criteria so they assess the capability you genuinely care about.

Example from practice

A rubric removes vague references to “professional tone” and instead specifies audience awareness, clarity and appropriate evidence use.
