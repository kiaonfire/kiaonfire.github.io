Quick fixes

1

Clarify the outcome

Write the genuine learning outcome before deciding the assessment format.

2

Show what success looks like

Provide annotated examples and explain how criteria apply.

3

Reduce hidden costs

Remove unnecessary travel, software, equipment or production expenses.

4

Review rigid conditions

Use strict time, place or format requirements only when they are essential.

A slightly meatier review

Review this area in five steps

  1. Write the intended learning outcome in one sentence and identify every task requirement that measures it.
  2. Identify untaught conventions, language demands or professional norms students are expected to infer.
  3. Check deadlines, format, attendance, technology and financial assumptions for avoidable barriers.
  4. Consider bounded choice, staged work, formative feedback or equivalent demonstration formats.
  5. Review whether criteria and moderation preserve the same standard across any options provided.

Barrier → response

Common barriers and practical responses

Single high-stakes submission

Add a proposal, draft, checkpoint or low-stakes practice opportunity before the final task.

Unclear or culturally narrow criteria

Use plain-language outcome-aligned criteria and review assumptions about what valid knowledge or professionalism looks like.

Strict deadline or exam condition

Check whether fixed timing is essential. Build reasonable baseline flexibility while retaining individual adjustments.

Format is unrelated to the outcome

Permit equivalent formats where appropriate and assess them with shared outcome-based criteria.

Example redesign

Before and after

Before

A single timed written task is used to assess analysis, but marks also depend on speed and an assumed academic writing style.

After

Students practise analysis before the final task, receive clear criteria and may use approved equivalent formats assessed against the same outcomes.
