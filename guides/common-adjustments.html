Student Access Plan focus

Address a common SAP adjustment.

Use this guide to implement an individual adjustment well and to identify where course design could reduce the same barrier for more students.

Two responsibilities

  1. Implement the individual adjustment reliably and respectfully.
  2. Use recurring adjustment themes as evidence for broader inclusive design.
Universal design does not replace Student Access Plans.

The aim is not to eliminate adjustments or reduce access to them. It is to stop repeatedly recreating barriers that can be removed from the course baseline, while retaining individual support where it remains necessary.

Common SAP adjustments and course-design responses

Time, attendance and flexibility

Extensions

Students may need additional time because health conditions or other access barriers affect when they can complete assessment.

Implement the adjustment: Make extension processes clear, accessible and low-friction. Avoid requiring unnecessary personal detail.

Design response: Review deadline clustering, task staging, notice periods and whether a short self-managed extension can be built into the course.

Related UQ framework areas: course-design-E, assessment-E

Breaks during longer activities

Students may need to pause, move, regulate symptoms, use medication or manage fatigue during longer classes, laboratories, placements or assessments.

Implement the adjustment: Schedule predictable breaks and explain how students can step out and return without public disclosure.

Design response: Review activity length, pacing and continuous-attendance assumptions across teaching, assessment and placements.

Related UQ framework areas: teaching-E, teaching-A, assessment-E

Additional time for in-class activities

Health, fatigue, pain, processing or communication differences may make tightly timed classroom activities an access barrier.

Implement the adjustment: Provide processing time, written prompts and a route to complete or revisit activities where speed is not the learning outcome.

Design response: Redesign pacing and participation so rapid performance is not treated as the default evidence of engagement.

Related UQ framework areas: teaching-E, teaching-A, teaching-L

Additional time for in-class assessments or presentations

Students may need additional time because health, processing, speech, mobility or assistive-technology use affects task completion.

Implement the adjustment: Implement the approved adjustment reliably and ensure the additional time works with room, technology and scheduling arrangements.

Design response: Review whether the assessment is unnecessarily speeded and whether the format measures the intended outcome.

Related UQ framework areas: teaching-A, assessment-E, assessment-A, assessment-L

Support for health-related absenteeism and missed marks

Students may miss classes or low-stakes activities because of episodic or fluctuating health conditions.

Implement the adjustment: Provide a clear route to essential materials and, where possible, an equivalent way to make up participation or marks.

Design response: Review whether attendance is being used as a proxy for learning and design equivalent access into the course baseline.

Temporary online participation or alternate tutorial attendance

Students may temporarily need online access or another class time because of health, treatment, mobility, transport or caring needs.

Implement the adjustment: Explain the process for temporary mode or tutorial changes and provide equivalent access where it is practicable.

Design response: Design course delivery so essential learning can survive temporary changes in location, mode or schedule.

Materials and communication

Advance access to learning materials

Advance access can support preparation, assistive technology, fatigue management, language processing and students balancing health or caring needs.

Implement the adjustment: Publish essential slides, readings, prompts and activity instructions as early as practicable.

Design response: Create a reliable publication schedule and accessible source-material workflow across the whole course.

Related UQ framework areas: course-design-A, course-design-L, teaching-E, teaching-A, teaching-L

Transcripts of audio, video and animated material

Transcripts support students who cannot reliably access audio, who use assistive technologies, or who need searchable and reviewable text.

Implement the adjustment: Provide accurate captions and a usable transcript for essential recorded media.

Design response: Build captions, transcripts and visual-description requirements into media commissioning and production workflows.

Related UQ framework areas: course-design-A, teaching-A

Accessible formats for materials

Students may need structured, selectable and reflowable text rather than scanned PDFs, image-only files or inaccessible third-party content.

Implement the adjustment: Provide accessible source files and an alternative when a required format cannot be made accessible.

Design response: Use accessible templates, quality assurance and ownership so remediation is not repeated one student at a time.

Related UQ framework areas: course-design-A, teaching-A

Additional processing time or advance notice before being called on

Students may need more time to process language, organise a response, use communication technology or manage anxiety and health symptoms.

Implement the adjustment: Provide questions in advance, use think time and avoid cold-calling students without an agreed process.

Design response: Use varied participation modes and design pacing so immediate verbal response is not the default measure of understanding.

Related UQ framework areas: teaching-E, teaching-A, teaching-L, assessment-L

Clear, direct, non-figurative language and step-by-step tasks

Students may need explicit language and clear sequencing because figurative, ambiguous or densely layered instructions create avoidable processing barriers.

Implement the adjustment: Use direct language, explicit verbs, clear steps and examples of the expected outcome.

Design response: Audit disciplinary language and hidden conventions across materials, teaching and assessment rather than simplifying only one task.

Related UQ framework areas: course-design-L, teaching-L, assessment-L

Written as well as verbal instructions

Students may not be able to reliably retain or process instructions delivered through speech alone.

Implement the adjustment: Provide key instructions, deadlines, changes and activity steps in writing as well as verbally.

Design response: Create consistent written communication and activity templates across the course and placements.

Participation and assessment

Adjustments to group work

Students may need alternative assessment, supported group allocation, structured processes, explicitly negotiated roles or alternative communication modes.

Implement the adjustment: Clarify roles, communication methods, contribution expectations and a private route for raising access or safety concerns.

Design response: Review whether collaboration is essential, teach collaboration explicitly, offer structured or alternative pathways and coordinate group-work burden across the program.

Related UQ framework areas: teaching-E, teaching-A, teaching-L, assessment-E, assessment-A

Adjustments to oral presentations

Students may require additional time, modified conditions or an equivalent assessment format when oral delivery creates a barrier unrelated to the learning outcome.

Implement the adjustment: Clarify which oral capabilities are genuinely assessed and provide accessible instructions, practice and reasonable conditions.

Design response: Offer equivalent demonstration modes when oral presentation is not essential, using shared outcome-aligned criteria.

Health and physical environment

Food, drink or medicine in class

Students may need immediate access to food, drink or medication to manage health safely.

Implement the adjustment: Communicate that medically required food, drink and medicine can be used without unnecessary attention, subject only to genuine safety restrictions.

Design response: Review blanket classroom and laboratory rules and create clear, dignified alternatives where safety restrictions genuinely apply.

Related UQ framework areas: teaching-E, teaching-A

Ergonomic desks, chairs, lab tables or stools

Students may require adjustable or supportive furniture to manage pain, fatigue, mobility or physical access.

Implement the adjustment: Confirm the required furniture is available, correctly located and does not segregate the student unnecessarily.

Design response: Audit common teaching, laboratory and placement spaces for flexible furniture and escalation pathways.

Related UQ framework areas: course-design-E, teaching-E, teaching-A

Predictable lighting and sensory environments

Lighting, sound, crowding, temperature, odours and unexpected sensory changes can prevent participation or worsen health symptoms.

Implement the adjustment: Communicate known conditions and changes, reduce unnecessary sensory load and identify quieter or lower-stimulation options where possible.

Design response: Include sensory access in room selection, teaching plans, events, laboratories and placement preparation.

Related UQ framework areas: course-design-E, teaching-E, teaching-A

Technology and human support

Screen readers and speech-to-text use

Students may rely on screen readers, speech recognition or speech-to-text for reading, navigation, writing and participation.

Implement the adjustment: Ensure documents, platforms, forms and assessment systems are keyboard-operable and compatible with assistive technology.

Design response: Test key course tasks with assistive technologies and provide accessible fallbacks for incompatible third-party tools.

Related UQ framework areas: course-design-A, teaching-A, assessment-A

Peer note takers, readers and scribes

Some students may require human support to access spoken, written or assessment content.

Implement the adjustment: Implement the support arrangement reliably and protect privacy, role clarity and access to necessary materials.

Design response: Improve advance materials, accessible recordings and course documentation while retaining human support where it remains necessary.

Placements and field learning

Priority placement allocation closer to home

Travel distance can create major access barriers through health, fatigue, transport, caring and financial pressures.

Implement the adjustment: Include approved access needs in placement allocation and communicate the process and constraints early.

Design response: Review placement-allocation criteria, travel expectations, transport costs and the availability of equitable alternatives.

Related UQ framework areas: course-design-E, teaching-E, assessment-E

Flexible placement timing

Fixed placement hours or blocks may conflict with health treatment, fatigue, caring responsibilities or accessible transport.

Implement the adjustment: Negotiate approved timing changes with the placement site and clearly document essential attendance requirements.

Design response: Design placement models with more than one viable schedule or pathway where accreditation and safety requirements allow.
