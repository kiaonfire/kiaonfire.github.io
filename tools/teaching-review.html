Teaching review

Review live and online teaching practice through the four dimensions: whose knowledge is welcomed, how participation works, whether delivery is accessible, and how language and norms are scaffolded.

Key tenet These dimensions interact. A student may face barriers across several categories simultaneously, and the strongest inclusive practice addresses them in combination rather than ticking each off independently. When using this tool, consider your course or teaching as a whole and look for patterns across dimensions rather than treating each row in isolation.

Use the levels as a shared review language, not as a rigid compliance score. Some areas may be stronger than others, and the goal is to identify where course design, teaching and assessment reinforce the same barrier.

Section 2: Teaching

What happens in lectures, tutorials, online sessions and placement supervision. The live, relational dimension of inclusive practice.

Review reminder: after selecting levels in this section, look across the four dimensions to see whether the same underlying barrier appears in more than one row.
R

Representation of, and respect for, cultures and identities

How teaching and supervision actively recognise, respect and invite diverse knowledges, identities and experiences.

No/Poor

Baseline gap

Country is not acknowledged; diverse identities remain invisible or othered; no diverse practitioner representation; placement supervision reflects only dominant cultural norms.

In practice: Country is not acknowledged, examples reproduce stereotypes and students from marginalised groups are expected to educate the class.

Developing

Some movement

Country and some cultural diversity are acknowledged inconsistently; students are occasionally invited to contribute and placement supervision is culturally aware in principle but not practice.

In practice: Inclusive language and acknowledgements are used inconsistently, while diverse contributions remain optional or dependent on confident individuals.

Proficient

Strong practice

Country is meaningfully acknowledged; the same intellectual standard is applied to culturally grounded contributions; diverse students are recognised as valuable knowledge holders.

In practice: Teaching actively recognises diverse knowledge, applies the same intellectual respect to culturally grounded contributions and addresses microaggressions.

Excellent

Embedded practice

Country is acknowledged consistently and meaningfully; a broad range of cultures and identities are welcomed; student feedback on inclusion is sought and acted on in both class and placement supervision.

In practice: Inclusive interaction is embedded across the teaching team, feedback is acted on and community or practitioner relationships shape delivery and supervision.

How to move this area forward

No/Poor → Developing
  • Review examples, humour and spontaneous language for stereotypes or deficit framing.
  • Acknowledge Country meaningfully and connect it to the discipline where appropriate.
  • Do not ask students to represent an entire identity or community.

Review examples, humour and spontaneous language for stereotypes or deficit framing. Acknowledge Country meaningfully and connect it to the discipline where appropriate. Do not ask students to represent an entire identity or community.

Developing → Proficient
  • Set and revisit clear norms for disagreement, respect and repair.
  • Use diverse practitioner and community voices as substantive teaching content.
  • Create a private route for students to raise exclusion or microaggressions.

Set and revisit clear norms for disagreement, respect and repair. Use diverse practitioner and community voices as substantive teaching content. Create a private route for students to raise exclusion or microaggressions.

Proficient → Excellent
  • Use student feedback to review classroom climate and supervision practice.
  • Support the whole teaching team to respond consistently to harmful behaviour.
  • Build sustained relationships rather than one-off guest appearances.

Use student feedback to review classroom climate and supervision practice. Support the whole teaching team to respond consistently to harmful behaviour. Build sustained relationships rather than one-off guest appearances.

Maintain, evaluate and share
  • Evaluate who speaks, leads and is treated as authoritative in live teaching.
  • Share teaching practices and support less experienced staff.
  • Maintain consent, payment and editorial input for community contributors.

UQ Indigenising Curriculum Guide

UQ’s guide provides a university framework for embedding Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander knowledges, perspectives and pedagogies in curriculum design and review.

Explore this example from The University of Queensland

Use external examples as inspiration and evidence—not as a model to copy without adapting to the UQ context.

E

Equity of access and participation

Whether teaching participation and support access account for varied schedules, health, finances, caring responsibilities and other conditions.

No/Poor

Baseline gap

Teaching assumes students are always present and unconstrained; support services are never explained; attendance expectations are inflexible.

In practice: Participation assumes consistent health, transport, work and caring conditions, while support is discussed only after a student misses class.

Developing

Some movement

Some support and flexibility are encouraged, but awareness of varied circumstances does not consistently reshape participation or attendance expectations.

In practice: Staff offer flexibility informally, but students cannot predict what is available or whether asking will create disadvantage.

Proficient

Strong practice

Help-seeking and support options are normalised; materials are available in advance and varying student circumstances are acknowledged when possible.

In practice: Materials, participation routes and help-seeking information are provided in advance, with flexibility applied consistently where possible.

Excellent

Embedded practice

Teaching is proactively structured around varied student realities; materials and supports are recorded and flexible; communications about participation are intentionally inclusive.

In practice: Teaching is planned around variable student realities, mainstreams common adjustments and escalates structural attendance or timetable barriers.

How to move this area forward

No/Poor → Developing
  • Publish support routes, attendance expectations and available flexibility clearly.
  • Provide essential materials and activity instructions in advance.
  • Identify one participation mark or attendance rule that can be replaced with evidence of learning.

Publish support routes, attendance expectations and available flexibility clearly. Provide essential materials and activity instructions in advance. Identify one participation mark or attendance rule that can be replaced with evidence of learning.

Developing → Proficient
  • Create consistent responses to health-related absence and temporary online participation.
  • Build predictable breaks and processing time into longer sessions.
  • Review whether students can make up missed low-stakes learning where possible.

Create consistent responses to health-related absence and temporary online participation. Build predictable breaks and processing time into longer sessions. Review whether students can make up missed low-stakes learning where possible.

Proficient → Excellent
  • Coordinate attendance and flexibility expectations across the teaching team.
  • Mainstream adjustments that repeatedly arise.
  • Escalate timetable, room and placement barriers beyond the individual class.

Coordinate attendance and flexibility expectations across the teaching team. Mainstream adjustments that repeatedly arise. Escalate timetable, room and placement barriers beyond the individual class.

Maintain, evaluate and share
  • Review whether flexibility is equitable across cohorts and locations.
  • Monitor barriers that remain despite the inclusive baseline.
  • Share effective arrangements across courses and programs.

Review whether flexibility is equitable across cohorts and locations. Monitor barriers that remain despite the inclusive baseline. Share effective arrangements across courses and programs.

Accessible and Inclusive Learning at Edinburgh

Edinburgh’s institutional approach combines universal design with specific adjustments offered as standard practice because they support many students.

Explore this example from The University of Edinburgh

Use external examples as inspiration and evidence—not as a model to copy without adapting to the UQ context.

A

Accessibility and agency of materials and learning pathways

Whether teaching delivery itself is accessible and whether students have more than one meaningful way to participate.

No/Poor

Baseline gap

Lectures or tutorials are not delivered accessibly; recordings or captions are absent; students must self-identify to receive adjustments and cannot shape how they participate.

In practice: Teaching relies on speech, visually dense slides and one participation mode, with access dependent on students requesting special arrangements.

Developing

Some movement

Some accessible delivery practices exist, but they depend on individual adjustment and are not consistent across activities or placement supervision.

In practice: Captions or recordings exist for some sessions, but access and participation options vary across staff and weeks.

Proficient

Strong practice

Lectures and tutorials are delivered accessibly; recordings or alternative routes are available; students have several meaningful participation modes without formal disclosure.

In practice: Accessible slides, captions, recordings or equivalents and varied participation modes are routine across most teaching.

Excellent

Embedded practice

Accessible delivery is standard across teaching modes; students can choose how they participate and where possible how learning unfolds; accessibility is normalised rather than exceptional.

In practice: Accessible delivery and student agency are built into rooms, technology, teaching plans and supervision rather than added after disclosure.

How to move this area forward

No/Poor → Developing
  • Enable captions, repeat audience questions and describe essential visual information.
  • Provide written instructions alongside verbal directions.
  • Offer a written or private contribution mode in addition to speaking live.

Enable captions, repeat audience questions and describe essential visual information. Provide written instructions alongside verbal directions. Offer a written or private contribution mode in addition to speaking live.

Developing → Proficient
  • Use a consistent accessible presentation and recording workflow.
  • Test how screen readers, speech-to-text and high zoom work with key activities.
  • Plan alternatives when a room or platform cannot support access.

Use a consistent accessible presentation and recording workflow. Test how screen readers, speech-to-text and high zoom work with key activities. Plan alternatives when a room or platform cannot support access.

Proficient → Excellent
  • Build access expectations into tutor and supervisor preparation.
  • Use student testing to review complete teaching tasks.
  • Escalate recurring room and platform barriers with evidence of impact.

Build access expectations into tutor and supervisor preparation. Use student testing to review complete teaching tasks. Escalate recurring room and platform barriers with evidence of impact.

Maintain, evaluate and share
  • Evaluate whether students can exercise agency without disclosure.
  • Share successful delivery standards across the program.
  • Keep technology, rooms and staff capability under recurring review.

Evaluate whether students can exercise agency without disclosure. Share successful delivery standards across the program. Keep technology, rooms and staff capability under recurring review.

UQ Auracast rollout in lecture theatres

UQ installed direct audio streaming technology across dozens of lecture theatres, enabling students to connect hearing aids or headphones without an individual microphone arrangement.

Explore this example from The University of Queensland

Use external examples as inspiration and evidence—not as a model to copy without adapting to the UQ context.

L

Language and literacies

How explicitly teaching surfaces hidden norms, introduces terminology and makes disciplinary communication possible.

No/Poor

Baseline gap

Hidden academic norms are never surfaced; there is one dominant participation mode and no adjustment for speech, language or processing differences.

In practice: Instructions are delivered verbally, jargon and figurative language go unexplained and students are called on without processing time.

Developing

Some movement

Some norms are explained and some alternatives offered, but support for language and literacy gaps remains partial and inconsistent.

In practice: Some terms and norms are explained, but clarity depends on the teacher or students asking for repetition.

Proficient

Strong practice

Key disciplinary norms and terminology are made visible; pace allows processing time; multiple participation modes are available for varied language backgrounds and communication preferences.

In practice: Key terms, expectations and communication norms are explicit, with written instructions, think time and several participation modes.

Excellent

Embedded practice

Hidden norms are consistently unpacked; structure and pace are designed with processing in mind; language development is treated as legitimate learning rather than an exception.

In practice: Language and processing access shape pacing, materials and teaching-team practice across the course and placement settings.

How to move this area forward

No/Poor → Developing
  • Write down key instructions and break complex activities into clear steps.
  • Provide questions or prompts before asking for immediate responses.
  • Replace unnecessary figurative or ambiguous wording with direct language.

Write down key instructions and break complex activities into clear steps. Provide questions or prompts before asking for immediate responses. Replace unnecessary figurative or ambiguous wording with direct language.

Developing → Proficient
  • Create reusable activity and communication templates.
  • Build deliberate processing pauses into discussion and explanation.
  • Teach the language of professional or disciplinary interaction explicitly.

Create reusable activity and communication templates. Build deliberate processing pauses into discussion and explanation. Teach the language of professional or disciplinary interaction explicitly.

Proficient → Excellent
  • Review language and pacing with students using varied communication strategies.
  • Align teaching language scaffolds with assessment and placement expectations.
  • Support tutors and supervisors to use the same clear practices.

Review language and pacing with students using varied communication strategies. Align teaching language scaffolds with assessment and placement expectations. Support tutors and supervisors to use the same clear practices.

Maintain, evaluate and share
  • Evaluate whether students can use varied modes without being seen as less capable.
  • Share language-access strategies across programs.
  • Keep hidden norms visible and open to challenge.

Evaluate whether students can use varied modes without being seen as less capable. Share language-access strategies across programs. Keep hidden norms visible and open to challenge.
