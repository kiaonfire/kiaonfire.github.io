Curriculum & program design · Quick how-to

Check representation across your course

Look beyond a single “diversity week” and check who is visible as an ordinary knowledge-maker, practitioner and possible future.

Framework practices used here

This how-to pulls together several parts of the Inclusive Teaching Framework rather than treating the issue as belonging to one area.

Respect

Quick steps

1
Scan readings, cases, examples and speakers across the whole semester.
2
Notice which groups appear only as problems, patients, clients or disadvantaged populations.
3
Add credible disciplinary and community voices where perspectives are narrow.
4
Check that representation is connected to core disciplinary questions rather than token content.

Use these framework practices

Respect & representation

Broaden whose knowledge and perspectives are represented

Review who appears across readings, examples and guest speakers over the full course or program, not just one week.

Respect & representation

Embed marginalised perspectives meaningfully

Build these perspectives into core concepts, not sidebars or optional extras.

Respect & representation

Build inclusive capabilities into learning outcomes

Decide which capabilities are genuinely part of good disciplinary practice.

Example in practice

A first-year course maps every author, case and guest speaker, then broadens the set of voices students encounter as ordinary disciplinary expertise.
