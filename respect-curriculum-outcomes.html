Do learning outcomes include inclusive capabilities where relevant?

Inclusive capabilities belong in learning outcomes when they are genuinely part of good disciplinary or professional practice.

Why this matters

Learning outcomes signal what a course regards as important. If capabilities such as working across difference, cultural capability, ethical judgement or communicating with diverse audiences matter in the discipline but remain implicit, they are easy to leave untaught or unassessed. Naming relevant capabilities helps students see their purpose and gives teaching teams something clear to teach, practise and assess.

Keep the discipline at the centre. The aim is not to add unrelated outcomes. Ask which capabilities are already part of competent, ethical and responsible practice in your field, then make them visible where they matter.

What this looks like

Common signs

  • Course outcomes focus only on technical knowledge even though professional practice also requires judgement, communication, ethical decision-making or working across difference.
  • Inclusive capabilities appear in teaching activities but are not named in the outcomes or assessment.
  • Students are expected to infer professional or cultural expectations without being given opportunities to practise them.

Put this into practice

Use student experience, existing UQ practice and ready-to-adapt tools to move from the idea to a change you can make in a course or program.

Start with the student experience

Student partnership work at UQ shows the value of making students contributors to curriculum rather than only recipients of it. For learning outcomes, ask what the outcome means to students, what evidence would show they had achieved it, and whether the wording reflects the capabilities they actually need.

Connect it to existing UQ work

You may not need to invent a new capability from scratch. Check whether the work already exists elsewhere at UQ and use it as the institutional or curriculum anchor for the change.

  • Graduate Statement and Graduate Attributes — Connect program and course outcomes to the broader capabilities UQ expects graduates to develop.
  • Planning for learning — Use constructive alignment to connect learning outcomes, activities and assessment.
  • Co-creating curriculum with students — Use student partnership to test whether outcomes, activities and assessment are meaningful and understandable.
  • Indigenising curriculum — Where relevant, connect outcomes to Indigenous knowledges, perspectives and culturally capable practice.

Copy and adapt a starting point

These are starting points, not required wording. Open the type that is useful, replace the brackets with language that is meaningful in your discipline, and keep only capabilities that are genuinely relevant.

Suggested wording

By the end of this course, students will be able to analyse [disciplinary problem] using perspectives from [relevant communities, cultures or knowledge traditions], and explain how those perspectives influence conclusions.

Suggested wording

By the end of this course, students will be able to work constructively with people who bring different experiences or perspectives and reflect on how those differences shape professional decision-making.

Suggested wording

By the end of this course, students will be able to identify cultural and ethical implications of [disciplinary practice] and justify an appropriate response for the people and context involved.

Suggested wording

By the end of this course, students will be able to communicate [disciplinary knowledge] to [different audiences or communities], adapting language, format and evidence to support understanding and participation.

Quick mapping check

Before adding a new outcome, check how it will actually live in the course. If you cannot identify where students learn, practise and demonstrate it, the outcome may remain aspirational.

How can I do it?

  • Review the current teaching or assessment design for avoidable barriers.
  • Check how students will understand, practise and demonstrate the intended learning.
  • Ask students what is working and what needs clarification.

Make inclusive capabilities explicit in learning outcomes where they are genuinely part of disciplinary or professional practice.

Make these changes

  • Identify an inclusive capability that genuinely matters in your discipline, such as ethical judgement or working across difference.
  • Write it as an observable outcome students can demonstrate.
  • Align an activity and assessment criterion so students can practise and show the capability.

Example in practice

At UQ, the Bachelor of Arts aligned program learning outcomes with the Graduate Attributes and mapped where those outcomes were taught and assessed.

Copy a starting point

By the end of this course, students will be able to [disciplinary action] while considering [relevant perspectives or communities], and justify [decision or approach] to [audience].
