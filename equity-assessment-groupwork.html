Equity of opportunity · Assessment

Structure group work for equitable participation

Use this Inclusive Teaching Framework practice to identify a specific barrier, understand what it can feel like for students, and make a practical change.

Framework focus

Theme: Equity of opportunity for full participation

Area: Assessment

Back to Assessment

Inclusive Teaching Framework statement

Group work is structured (role rotation, agreements) to prevent reproducing inequities

Why this matters

The same course requirement can land very differently depending on time, money, care, disability, work, housing or confidence. Equity asks whether students with different lives have a real opportunity to participate and succeed, rather than simply receiving the same rule.

How the barrier can show up

  • Requirements may assume spare time, predictable schedules, money, transport or uninterrupted study conditions.
  • Support or flexibility may exist only after a student discloses private circumstances.
  • One difficult week can become a major barrier when recovery is not built in.

What it can feel like for students

  • “This course assumes university is the only thing happening in my life.”
  • “I can technically enrol, but the way this is organised makes participation fragile.”
  • “Asking for flexibility feels like admitting that I am not the right kind of student.”

How to address it in practice

1
Set clear roles and expectations rather than leaving everything to social negotiation.
2
Build in checkpoints and escalation routes.
3
Consider how availability, confidence and prior experience may shape who does what.

Example from practice

A group project includes role planning, a team agreement and a midpoint reset so workload and communication can be recalibrated.
