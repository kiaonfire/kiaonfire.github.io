Want to use the Inclusive Teaching Framework more holistically?

Explore the four framework areas below. They can be remembered as REAL: Respect, Equity, Accessibility and Language. Work through one area or scan all four as part of a course review.

RespectEquityAccessibilityLanguage
Respect & representation

Respect for, and representation of, cultures and identities

Check whose identities, knowledge, norms and ways of participating are treated as legitimate.

Back to top ↑

Set clear norms for respectful participation

Co-create or clearly state discussion norms early.

Treat varied life experience as an asset

Invite different kinds of examples, applications and experiences into discussion.

Address microaggressions when they arise

Prepare a simple response line so harmful comments do not go unaddressed.

Equity of opportunity

Equity of opportunity for full participation

Check whether students with different lives have a realistic opportunity to participate and succeed.

Back to top ↑

Make help-seeking normal and visible

Explain where students can go for help and why using support is normal.

Release key materials early

Share key slides, readings or guiding questions in advance where possible.

Notice disengagement and reach out early

Notice patterns early rather than waiting for failure.

Accessibility

Accessibility in physical and digital spaces

Check whether materials, environments and activities are usable without unnecessary friction.

Back to top ↑

Build processing time into teaching

Pause after complex explanations and allow time to think.

Use multimodal teaching routinely

Present essential information in more than one way where appropriate.

Offer different ways to participate

Offer more than one way to contribute, such as speaking, posting or sharing through smaller groups.

Language & literacies

Language, literacies and the hidden curriculum

Make academic and disciplinary language, rules and expectations visible and learnable.

Back to top ↑

Normalise unfamiliarity with academic language and norms

Invite students to ask about unfamiliar terms without embarrassment.

Make disciplinary conventions visible

Show what the convention looks like and why it exists.

Support students working in an additional language

Provide extra processing time where possible.

Teach the digital literacies students need

Identify which digital practices students are expected to use, from databases to citation tools.
