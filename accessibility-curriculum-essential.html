Accessibility · Course & Program Design

Make essential and optional content clear

Use this Inclusive Teaching Framework practice to identify a specific barrier, understand what it can feel like for students, and make a practical change.

Framework focus

Theme: Accessibility in physical and digital spaces

Area: Course & Program Design

Back to Course & Program Design

Inclusive Teaching Framework statement

Course materials clearly distinguish essential from optional content, so students with limited time or energy can prioritise important content or activities

Why this matters

Accessibility is about usability, not just technical availability. A resource, room or activity can exist while still costing some students far more energy to perceive, navigate, understand or act on. Repeated friction sends a message about who the default user is.

How the barrier can show up

  • Key information or activities may be hard to perceive, navigate or complete in the available format or environment.
  • Students may need to ask repeatedly for versions or arrangements that could have been built in.
  • The effort needed just to access the learning can compete with the effort needed to learn.

What it can feel like for students

  • “The information exists, but finding and using it takes more effort than learning it.”
  • “I have to keep asking for a version I can actually use.”
  • “I can enter the space, but I cannot participate in it on equal terms.”

How to address it in practice

1
Label core tasks and readings clearly.
2
Avoid making students guess what matters most.
3
Use structure to help students re-enter after disruption.

Example from practice

Each weekly module lists “Start here”, “Essential”, and “Explore further”, helping students focus when time or energy is tight.
