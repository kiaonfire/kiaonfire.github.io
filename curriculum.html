Run a quick inclusive course check

Use a short scan to find the most obvious inclusion barriers before you begin a deeper review.

Framework area: Respectespect · Equity · Accessibility · Language

Check your learning outcomes

Make sure outcomes describe the capabilities students actually need and do not hide unnecessary cultural or disciplinary assumptions.

Framework area: Respectespect · Language · Accessibility

Check representation across your course

Look beyond a single “diversity week” and check who is visible as an ordinary knowledge-maker, practitioner and possible future.

Framework area: Respect

Check workload, timing and hidden costs

Find course features that make participation harder because of time, money, travel or competing responsibilities rather than academic capability.

Framework area: Equity

Make the hidden curriculum visible

Identify the academic, disciplinary and digital rules students currently have to infer through trial and error.

Want to use the Inclusive Teaching Framework more holistically?

Explore the four framework areas below. They can be remembered as REAL: Respect, Equity, Accessibility and Language. Work through one area or scan all four as part of a course review.

RespectEquityAccessibilityLanguage
Respect & representation

Respect for, and representation of, cultures and identities

Check whose identities, knowledge, norms and ways of participating are treated as legitimate.

Back to top ↑

Broaden whose knowledge and perspectives are represented

Review who appears across readings, examples and guest speakers over the full course or program, not just one week.

Embed marginalised perspectives meaningfully

Build these perspectives into core concepts, not sidebars or optional extras.

Build inclusive capabilities into learning outcomes

Decide which capabilities are genuinely part of good disciplinary practice.

Equity of opportunity

Equity of opportunity for full participation

Check whether students with different lives have a realistic opportunity to participate and succeed.

Back to top ↑

Design for the students actually in your course

Review common barriers from prior offerings and likely cohort patterns before the semester begins.

Build flexibility into course structure and timelines

Check the weekly load, not just the total workload.

Check and reduce hidden course costs

Audit costs beyond tuition, including software, travel, printing, uniforms or materials.

Accessibility

Accessibility in physical and digital spaces

Check whether materials, environments and activities are usable without unnecessary friction.

Back to top ↑

Check materials and learning environments for accessibility

Adopt accessible document and media practices as standard workflow.

Make essential and optional content clear

Label core tasks and readings clearly.

Build common accessibility adjustments in by default

Identify the supports many students may need and design them in from the start.

Language & literacies

Language, literacies and the hidden curriculum

Make academic and disciplinary language, rules and expectations visible and learnable.

Back to top ↑

Teach the literacies students need

Treat reading, writing, speaking and disciplinary interpretation as teachable parts of the course.

Make terminology and expectations explicit

Name what key terms mean in the discipline and why they matter.

Build language support into the course

Create resources that any student can use without disclosing difficulty.
