Equity of opportunity · Course & Program Design

Have I reduced hidden course costs?

A practical Inclusive Teaching Framework action.

Framework practice

Equity · Curriculum

Financial barriers are minimised using library/free alternatives; unavoidable costs are flagged early

Back to Curriculum

Why this matters

The same course requirement can land very differently depending on time, money, care, disability, work, housing or confidence. Equity asks whether students with different lives have a real opportunity to participate and succeed, rather than simply receiving the same rule.

What this looks like

Common signs

  • Requirements may assume spare time, predictable schedules, money, transport or uninterrupted study conditions.
  • Support or flexibility may exist only after a student discloses private circumstances.
  • One difficult week can become a major barrier when recovery is not built in.

What students may experience

  • “This course assumes university is the only thing happening in my life.”
  • “I can technically enrol, but the way this is organised makes participation fragile.”
  • “Asking for flexibility feels like admitting that I am not the right kind of student.”

Steps to address it

1
Audit costs beyond tuition, including software, travel, printing, uniforms or materials.
2
Use free or library-supported options wherever possible.
3
Where costs are unavoidable, give students early notice and advice about support.

Example in practice

A design subject switches from proprietary software licences to institutionally supported tools and flags material costs in the course profile.
