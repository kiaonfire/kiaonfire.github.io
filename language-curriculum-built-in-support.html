Build language support into the course

Use this Inclusive Teaching Framework practice to identify a specific barrier, understand what it can feel like for students, and make a practical change.

Framework focus

Theme: Language, literacies and the hidden curriculum

Area: Course & Program Design

Back to Course & Program Design

Inclusive Teaching Framework statement

Built-in language support (glossaries, worked examples) is available to all, without needing to self-identify

Why this matters

University contains many hidden rules: what “critical analysis” means, what a rubric is really asking for, when it is acceptable to ask for help, or what counts as “professional” behaviour. If those rules remain unspoken, familiarity can be mistaken for capability.

How the barrier can show up

  • Important expectations may remain implicit or be expressed only through insider terminology.
  • Students may be expected to infer processes or disciplinary conventions through trial and error.
  • Assessment can feel like a test of hidden rules rather than a supported opportunity to learn.

What it can feel like for students

  • “Everyone else seems to know rules nobody has explained.”
  • “I understand the topic, but not what I am being asked to do.”
  • “I keep learning how university works only after I have already made the mistake.”

How to address it in practice

1
Create resources that any student can use without disclosing difficulty.
2
Place support at the point of need rather than elsewhere on a separate website.
3
Normalise using glossaries, exemplars and worked examples.

Example from practice

Each weekly module includes a short glossary and one worked example showing how to approach the most demanding task.
