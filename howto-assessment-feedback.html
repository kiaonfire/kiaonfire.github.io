Assessment · Quick how-to

Make feedback useful and accessible

Give students feedback they can understand, access and use in their next piece of work.

Framework practices used here

This how-to pulls together several parts of the Inclusive Teaching Framework rather than treating the issue as belonging to one area.

RespectAccessibilityLanguage

Quick steps

1
Identify the most important next move rather than listing every weakness.
2
Use respectful language that treats the student as a developing learner.
3
Offer accessible feedback modes where feasible.
4
Create a later opportunity to apply the feedback.

Use these framework practices

Respect & representation

Give respectful, intellectually serious feedback

Write feedback as if speaking to a capable learner, not sorting them into worthiness categories.

Accessibility

Offer accessible ways to receive feedback

Offer feedback in more than one mode where feasible, such as written comments plus an audio summary.

Language & literacies

Use feedback to develop communication

Comment on how effectively meaning is being made, not only surface errors.

Example in practice

A student receives the rubric, short written priorities and an optional audio explanation, then applies the feedback in the next assessment.
