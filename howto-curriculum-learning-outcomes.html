Curriculum & program design · Quick how-to

Check your learning outcomes

Make sure outcomes describe the capabilities students actually need and do not hide unnecessary cultural or disciplinary assumptions.

Framework practices used here

This how-to pulls together several parts of the Inclusive Teaching Framework rather than treating the issue as belonging to one area.

RespectLanguageAccessibility

Quick steps

1
Underline the capability each outcome is really asking students to develop.
2
Check whether any wording assumes unexplained professional or disciplinary norms.
3
Ask whether inclusive or intercultural capability is genuinely part of good practice in the discipline.
4
Check that assessment can measure the outcome without requiring irrelevant modes or conventions.

Use these framework practices

Respect & representation

Build inclusive capabilities into learning outcomes

Decide which capabilities are genuinely part of good disciplinary practice.

Language & literacies

Make terminology and expectations explicit

Name what key terms mean in the discipline and why they matter.

Respect & representation

Check cultural assumptions and hidden norms

Check whether the task rewards one style of expression when that style is not the learning outcome.

Accessibility

Offer meaningful choice in assessment

Offer choice where it supports engagement and does not compromise outcomes.

Example in practice

A program rewrites a vague “professional communication” outcome to specify audience awareness, evidence use and clear explanation, making the capability easier to teach and assess.
