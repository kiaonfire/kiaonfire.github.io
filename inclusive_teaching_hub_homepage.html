Why this matters

Better design helps everyone do better work.

Inclusive teaching is educational quality, not a side project.

FOR STUDENTS

Fewer avoidable barriers

Students spend less energy repeatedly explaining, disclosing and negotiating for access.

FOR STAFF

Less repeated repair

Clearer materials and flexible defaults reduce the need to solve the same problem one student at a time.

FOR UQ

More people can belong

Students can see themselves as legitimate future engineers, clinicians, teachers, researchers and leaders.

UDL in plain language

Four questions. Use them everywhere.

Universal Design for Learning helps us design for learner variability from the beginning.

01

Engage

Can students understand why this matters and connect with it in more than one way?

02

Access

Can students perceive, understand and revisit the information they need?

03

Demonstrate

Does the task measure the intended learning rather than an unrelated barrier?

04

Belong

Can different students see their knowledge, identities and futures taken seriously?

Barrier check

The same adjustment keeps coming up.

A recurring adjustment can be useful evidence about course design. It may point to something that can be improved for everyone.

Universal design does not replace individual adjustments. Student Access Plans and reasonable adjustments remain essential. The aim is to remove avoidable barriers before students encounter them.
Students repeatedly need extensions

Check assessment clustering, task clarity, staged submissions and whether a short self-managed extension could be built in.

Students cannot participate in live discussion

Offer written, asynchronous, anonymous or small-group options where speaking live is not the learning outcome.

Students need alternative document formats

Create accessible source documents with headings, descriptive links, usable tables and appropriate image descriptions.

Students are unclear about assessment expectations

Provide annotated exemplars, plain-language criteria, staged practice and explicit teaching of unfamiliar disciplinary conventions.

Quick guides

Small changes with a clear purpose.

ACCESS

Make materials easier to use

  • Use meaningful headings.
  • Caption essential video and audio.
  • Write descriptive link text.
  • Provide downloadable formats.
ASSESSMENT

Measure the learning—not the barrier

  • Identify the genuine learning outcome.
  • Teach unfamiliar conventions explicitly.
  • Provide practice before high stakes.
  • Remove unnecessary time pressure.
PARTICIPATION

Make more than one doorway

  • Share questions in advance.
  • Offer verbal and written options.
  • Use individual and group activities.
  • Explain how students can ask for help.
BELONGING

Help students see a future for themselves

Review who appears as an expert, leader or innovator. Separate genuine professional capabilities from inherited expectations about confidence, accent, clothing, gender, disability, personality, culture or class.

Students should be able to develop as professionals without first becoming a narrow version of the “ideal” engineer, clinician, teacher or academic.
