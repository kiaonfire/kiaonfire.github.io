Want to use the Inclusive Teaching Framework more holistically?

Explore the four framework areas below. They can be remembered as REAL: Respect, Equity, Accessibility and Language. Work through one area or scan all four as part of a course review.

RespectEquityAccessibilityLanguage
Respect & representation

Respect for, and representation of, cultures and identities

Check whose identities, knowledge, norms and ways of participating are treated as legitimate.

Back to top ↑

Check cultural assumptions and hidden norms

Check whether the task rewards one style of expression when that style is not the learning outcome.

Offer different ways to demonstrate learning

Offer equivalent formats where the mode itself is not essential.

Give respectful, intellectually serious feedback

Write feedback as if speaking to a capable learner, not sorting them into worthiness categories.

Equity of opportunity

Equity of opportunity for full participation

Check whether students with different lives have a realistic opportunity to participate and succeed.

Back to top ↑

Build common adjustments into assessment

Design short extensions, alternative participation routes or recovery options as normal features where appropriate.

Structure group work for equitable participation

Set clear roles and expectations rather than leaving everything to social negotiation.

Build in drafts and checkpoints

Add low-stakes checkpoints that make progress visible before the final due date.

Make marking and grade queries transparent

Explain how marking works, what moderation means and how students can query results.

Accessibility

Accessibility in physical and digital spaces

Check whether materials, environments and activities are usable without unnecessary friction.

Back to top ↑

Offer meaningful choice in assessment

Offer choice where it supports engagement and does not compromise outcomes.

Make assessment criteria and materials accessible

Check rubrics, exemplars and task sheets for clarity and accessibility.

Offer accessible ways to receive feedback

Offer feedback in more than one mode where feasible, such as written comments plus an audio summary.

Language & literacies

Language, literacies and the hidden curriculum

Make academic and disciplinary language, rules and expectations visible and learnable.

Back to top ↑

Write clear assessment instructions and criteria

Write instructions in plain, direct language without losing disciplinary precision.

Use low-stakes practice before high-stakes assessment

Let students practise unfamiliar genres before they are graded heavily.

Use annotated exemplars to show what good looks like

Provide more than a finished sample by annotating what makes it effective.

Use feedback to develop communication

Comment on how effectively meaning is being made, not only surface errors.
